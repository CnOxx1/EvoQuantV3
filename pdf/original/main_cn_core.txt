Abstract: This paper studies an overlooked but fundamental issue in AI-based financial applications: for an intelligent system that continuously interprets market states and produces auditable judgments, its performance frontier is often constrained less by

model complexity than by whether the market world presented to it is sufficiently broad, stable, and honest. Motivated by the proposition that many so-called AI trading systems fail not because the model is not smart enough, but because the market worldview fed into the model is too thin, this paper does not treat the current project as a return predictor or an automated trading engine. Instead, it conceptualizes the project as a data world model infrastructure for AI-based market analysis. Combining project documentation, code structure, database semantics, and logic-layer bundle construction, the paper develops a formal framework that maps latent market states to AI-visible world objects. It then defines world model quality along three dimensions--breadth, stability, and honesty--and proposes evaluative objects such as the World Model Index, explanation volatility, unsupported claim rate, stale-input sensitivity, and evidence attribution rate. The analysis suggests that the core value of the project lies not in directly generating trading answers, but in reducing world-model error, compressing spurious explanatory degrees of freedom, and improving the auditability and institutional usability of AI conclusions through latest_* snapshots, quality gating, the separation of main and diagnostic views, bundle aggregation across eight evidence bands, and temporal governance. The paper contributes by elevating the notion of a "market world model" from an engineering narrative to a formal, identifiable, and evaluable academic object.
Key words:AI market world model; cryptocurrency; data infrastructure; market microstructure; information set; quality governance
